Same bet.
Two prices.

The same outcome often trades on World, Kalshi and Polymarket at once. Half the time World is within about half a cent of the best price, and nine times in ten within 1.6¢. I built a PayBox plugin so your agent pays a cent to catch the ones that aren't, before it buys.

    This preview is free and needs no wallet. It asks Kalshi and Polymarket live, and uses World's price from my last snapshot (16 Oct, 11:43 UTC). Inside PayBox, your agent sends World's live price and pays 0.01 USDC.

    01 · Inside PayBox

    How your agent
    uses it

    Second Opinion is a PayBox plugin. Your agent calls it right before it buys a YES on World. Each check costs one cent.

    1. STEP 1

      Find the outcome

      Your agent finds the market and its live YES price with PayBox's World plugin.

    2. STEP 2

      Ask for a second opinion

      It calls Second Opinion's second_opinion tool with the World ticker and that price.

      second_opinion({
  world_ticker: "WXPRESWIN-28-GNEW",
  world_yes_ask: 0.1055
})
    3. STEP 3

      Approve one cent

      PayBox shows a 0.01 USDC approval. You approve with your passkey. The receipt comes back, and the agent buys, waits, or sets a lower price.

    02 · Measured on 793 outcomes

    The number

    I matched every World outcome I could to the same outcome on Kalshi or Polymarket. Then I measured how far World's YES price sat above the cheapest one. Most of the time the gap is small.

    793World outcomes matched
    0.56¢median premium, before fees
    1.56¢90th percentile
    49outcomes 2¢ or more over

    Kalshi and Polymarket charge buyers a taker fee on top of their price. World doesn't, because its cost is already in the quote. With those fees added:

    408of 793 outcomes where World is the cheapest after fees
    −0.05¢median premium after fees
    28still 2¢ or more over after fees

    On the 49 outcomes that are 2¢ or more over before fees, a buyer pays a median of about $20 more per $100 than on the cheaper venue.

    Method. World quotes come from the PayBox World plugin. Kalshi and Polymarket quotes come from their public APIs. I only compare the same outcome with the same resolution period, and I leave out the 23 pairs where Kalshi and Polymarket disagree by more than 3¢, because that usually means the rules differ. The verdict compares YES asks. The after-fees view adds Kalshi's taker fee (0.07 × p × (1 − p) per share) and Polymarket's (its category rate, 0 to 0.07, × p × (1 − p)), using World's category for Polymarket's; Kalshi's rounding up to the cent per order is left out.
